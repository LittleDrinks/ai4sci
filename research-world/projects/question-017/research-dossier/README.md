# Q017 · How is immune homeostasis maintained and regulated?

Immune homeostasis is a state of internal equilibrium. Without homeostasis, the immune system cannot function correctly, leading to disease and infection. Knowing how this balance is maintained can provide a fuller picture of how the body works and may help us in calibrating medical interventions. For instance, instead of nonselectively boosting or suppressing the whole immune system as we currently do, in the future we may be able to rebalance a dysregulated immune system. Studies in humans and in animal models have pursued almost every aspect of immune response and examined many immune cell populations, yet several unanswered questions remain. Genomic, proteomic, and other ‘omics data are helpful. But the human immune system is dynamic and very individualized, making animal models of limited use—thus a robust platform to study and manipulate human immune cells is needed to address the problem.

## Direction
Human immune-cell perturbation platform

Establishes a high-fidelity ex vivo and organoid-based experimental framework to directly manipulate human immune networks, bypassing species-specific translational gaps inherent in animal models.

## Learned
- Synthetic hydrogel-based lymphoid organoids can successfully sustain germinal center reactions and model human B-cell programming.
- PBMC-derived constructs demonstrate potential longevity advantages over tonsil-derived equivalents for maintaining plasma cell populations.
- Concurrent chemical inhibition of EZH2 and BCL6 is hypothesized to synergistically drive terminal plasma cell differentiation beyond single-target blockade.
- The current evidence base is fragmented, consisting primarily of publication metadata, paywalled abstracts, and extraction failures rather than validated experimental protocols.

## Evidence
- Qualitatively supports the use of engineered hydrogels to mimic lymphoid microenvironments for controlled B-cell response modeling.
- Demonstrates differential therapeutic responses (e.g., anti-CD20 impairment) and microenvironmental sensitivity between healthy donors and lymphoma patients.
- Validates the technical feasibility of multi-parameter mass cytometry and transcriptomic profiling to track hematopoietic continuum dynamics post-perturbation.
- Indicates that targeted epigenetic/transcriptional modulation can influence germinal center architecture and plasma cell output in vitro.

## Limitations
- Cannot substantiate comprehensive immune network manipulation due to a narrow focus on B-cells and absence of T-cell/innate lymphoid data.
- Lacks the validated standard operating procedures, dose-response matrices, viability controls, and predefined signaling fidelity thresholds mandated by the completion test.
- Fails to account for donor-to-donor immunological variability, ex vivo expansion artifacts, and potential off-target cytotoxicity without empirical wet-lab execution.
- Dry-lab simulations and computational models cannot replicate the complex biophysical constraints of hydrogel matrices or real-time epigenetic-transcriptional crosstalk.

## Open Questions
- What are the precise molar ratios and dosing kinetics for concurrent EZH2/BCL6 inhibition that maximize synergy while maintaining >60% cell viability?
- How does inter-donor variability impact organoid reproducibility, and what statistical power metrics are required to normalize survival curves?
- Can polarized CXCL12 gradients or other microenvironmental cues overcome anti-CD20-induced suppression in lymphoma-derived B-cell niches?
- Which specific multi-parameter readouts and viability-correction algorithms best define 'signaling fidelity' across heterogeneous primary isolates?

## Next Moves
- Secure IRB approval and procure matched healthy/lymphoma donor peripheral blood mononuclear cells for cohort expansion.
- Develop and internally validate the wet-lab SOP, establishing titrated dose-response matrices, vehicle controls, and standardized viability normalization protocols.
- Execute bench-scale hydrogel organoid cultures with EZH2/BCL6 perturbations, capturing multi-parameter flow cytometry and transcriptomic endpoints.
- Apply formal synergy quantification models (Bliss Independence, Chou-Talalay) and pre-study power calculations to generate definitive success thresholds.
- Temporarily narrow the directional scope to B-cell lymphoid organoids pending acquisition of broader immune network validation data.
